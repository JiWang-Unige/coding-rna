# Completed TRAIN continuations and reference-hit changes

## Complete-CDS-chain accuracy

The [accuracy figure](render_r1/train_continuation_complete_chain.svg) shows the initial C0 model, all eight completed final continuations and the three fixed native comparators on the original six TRAIN chromosomes. Panels A and B contain three Arabidopsis thaliana and three Oryza sativa chromosomes, with 17,739 and 4,949 primary references; panel C pools all six chromosomes and 22,688 references. F1 is `100 × 2TP / (predicted + reference)`, computed separately from each scope's counts. Pooled F1 is not the average of species or chromosome scores. Labels are rounded to one decimal place; [provenance.json](render_r1/provenance.json) retains unrounded precision, recall, F1 and the underlying counts for all 36 bars.

Method order follows the declared branches, not a performance ranking. C0 uses the fixed final 4,608-update weights; all continuations use their fixed final 9,216-update weights. Native tools retain their existing operating points. Blue bars denote model branches, the hatched blue bar denotes C0, and gray bars denote native tools. Matching requires the sequence identifier, strand and all ordered CDS intervals to agree. Phase consistency is assessed separately; difficult and grammar-unsupported references remain in the denominator, and nonprimary isoform hits are not primary true positives.

Joint 24,576-base encoding exceeded Helixer in both species on this TRAIN panel, but remained below ANNEVO and Tiberius. Combining joint encoding with native CDS priors did not preserve the context-only gain. These are single-fit TRAIN measurements, not independent generalization, a matched training-budget comparison, a causal mechanism or DEV-based model selection. Upstream annotation exposure and effective optimization differ across methods. The combined and complete-core models are shown as completed TRAIN branches, not added to the frozen DEV candidates.

## Reference hits gained and lost

The [paired reference-hit figure](render_r1/train_continuation_reference_gain_loss.svg) compares seven changed final-9,216 models with the matched structured continuation. The three panels retain the same species and pooled reference populations. Filled blue bars to the right count references missed by the control but recovered by the candidate; open, orange-hatched bars to the left count references recovered by the control but missed by the candidate. The printed lost counts are positive magnitudes; the signed axis distinguishes losses from gains. The net column is gained minus lost. All three panels use the same count axis; their reference population sizes differ.

Against structured continuation, joint encoding gained 2,956 and lost 726 pooled references, for a net increase of 2,230 hits. Native priors, fresh uniform sampling, the combined branch and complete-core sampling also had positive pooled net changes, whereas local and mixed continuation had negative pooled net changes. Gains and losses remain visible separately rather than being replaced by a net-only comparison. These quantities describe recall accounting on annotated primary references, not discovery of new genes, subset prediction precision or F1, statistical significance or causal attribution. Their pooled counts equal the sums of the two species. Provenance retains all 21 partitions, including retained hits and still-missed references.
