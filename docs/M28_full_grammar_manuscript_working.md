## Abstract

Ab initio annotation requires complete coding sequence (CDS) chains, not coding-base detection alone. We develop a sequence-only framework that learns from partial observations by summing over compatible complete gene sets, including the empty set. Fine observations restrict structural roles, coarse labels contribute an observation channel, and unknown positions are marginalized. Overlapping neural-window scores support whole-contig decoding under DNA and reading-frame constraints. On two historically reused Arabidopsis and rice development chromosomes containing 7,728 primary reference chains, a predeclared fresh-sampling and joint-encoding model reaches pooled exact-chain F1 of 70.107%, versus 62.851% for Helixer, 71.805% for Tiberius and 75.703% for ANNEVO. Reference-informed diagnostics distinguish grammar exclusion from positive-energy chains outscored by conflicts. Coarse-channel effects remain species-dependent, and codon energies reduce complete-chain accuracy. The low-rank/full-rank ordering reverses between two tested learning rates in TRAIN-only controls. Single-fit measurements, reused development data and upstream annotation exposure limit interpretation: independently validated superiority and broader applicability remain unestablished.

## Introduction

Structural gene annotation connects a genome assembly to the protein-coding sequences it contains. Sequence-only prediction is particularly useful when RNA sequencing or homologous protein evidence is unavailable, but its output must resolve more than the presence of coding bases. A complete CDS chain requires the correct strand, every coding-exon boundary and a consistent reading frame. A prediction can therefore achieve high nucleotide-level accuracy while still failing to reconstruct the complete protein-coding structure. Modern annotation studies accordingly distinguish base-wise measurements from exact feature and gene-model accuracy. [Helixer](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41592-025-02939-1), [Tiberius](https://academic.oup.com/bioinformatics/article/40/12/btae685/7903281).

Neural sequence models and biological structure constraints already underpin strong ab initio predictors. Helixer predicts genic classes and coding phase with convolutional and recurrent networks, then uses HelixerPost to reconstruct primary gene models within candidate regions. Tiberius incorporates a differentiable HMM into neural fine-tuning and inference, rather than restricting the HMM to postprocessing. ANNEVO combines convolutional compression, long-range Transformer representations and expert routing with Viterbi-based structure decoding. These methods establish that local sequence evidence, long-range context and gene grammar can be combined effectively; adding one of these ingredients is not sufficient to establish a new contribution. [Helixer Methods](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41592-025-02939-1), [Tiberius Methods](https://academic.oup.com/bioinformatics/article/40/12/btae685/7903281), [ANNEVO Supplementary Methods 2 and 3](https://media.springernature.com/original/springer-static/esm/art%3A10.1038%2Fs41592-026-03036-7/MediaObjects/41592_2026_3036_MOESM1_ESM.pdf).

The relevant comparison is also no longer limited to the original mammalian Tiberius model. Its 2026 multi-clade preprint describes lineage-specific models beyond mammals, including flowering plants, fungi, insects, green algae and diatoms. Broader applicability must therefore be measured against the actual model versions and clades available, rather than inferred from an older method description. This preprint is a source for the expanded model family, not a replacement for our fixed operating points or a source of scores to import into our own benchmark. [Tiberius multi-clade preprint](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42094444/).

Discriminative learning over gene structures also has a substantial history. Conrad uses semi-Markov conditional random fields to combine evidence within a discriminatively trained gene predictor. More recently, GeneDecoder has coupled learned DNA representations with a latent CRF, negative-log-likelihood training and Viterbi prediction, including experiments with frozen pretrained embeddings and labels that do not explicitly specify gene direction. The Carbon-A technical report likewise describes a complete annotation pipeline in which strand-specific coding probabilities feed a transition network, a sequence-constrained Viterbi decoder and a gene-confidence network, producing GFF3 and CDS/protein FASTA outputs. These precedents preclude treating structured likelihood, latent roles or pretrained representations followed by a gene decoder as intrinsically novel. [Conrad](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/17690204/), [GeneDecoder Methods and experiments](https://arxiv.org/html/2505.03377v1), [Carbon-A technical report pages 24 and 25](https://huggingface.co/buckets/HuggingFaceBio/Carbon-A-Technical-Report/resolve/Carbon-A.pdf).

DNA language models have also been adapted directly to gene annotation. GENATATOR combines a fine-tuned GENA-LM, recurrent memory and a nucleotide-resolution U-Net head, and evaluates exact exon reconstruction separately from exact CDS reconstruction. Its annotation scope includes noncoding transcripts and untranslated regions, rather than only protein-coding CDS chains. Long-context language-model annotation and nucleotide-resolution output therefore cannot themselves distinguish our framework. Conversely, our CDS-only output is not broader in feature coverage; any claim of broader applicability must be established on an independently evaluated biological scope. [GENATATOR architecture and benchmarks](https://openreview.net/pdf?id=ouMoBqgjfj).

The question we investigate is whether training competition over complete, compatible CDS structures can improve sequence-only annotation when supervision does not resolve every structural role. Resolved role observations, coarse region observations and genuinely unknown positions carry different information. Filling unknown positions with background would change that learning problem, while improving an intermediate base classifier need not improve the final CDS chain. Likewise, a decoder can only choose among structures represented by its grammar and supported by its supplied scores; exact optimization within that universe is not proof of biological completeness or learned accuracy.

Our implemented framework combines a frozen annotation-pretrained DNA encoder with a trainable role-scoring head. Within finite training views, it optimizes the likelihood of partial fine/coarse observations by summing over permitted complete gene sets, including the empty set, rather than restricting competition to an enumerated reference-informed candidate pool. Inference instead receives genomic FASTA and a fixed final checkpoint. Base scores from overlapping neural windows are assigned by a fixed ownership rule, and one complete-set MAP solve is applied to each entire oriented contig under literal DNA and reading-frame constraints. Training remains a collection of finite-view likelihoods, not chromosome-joint maximum likelihood; whole-contig decoding does not erase that distinction. The [Methods](#methods) gives the precise scoring, observation channel and structural scope.

The empirical test is complete CDS chain performance under a common input and evaluation policy, supplemented by base coverage and background burden. The initial TRAIN comparison showed that relatively high CDS-base accuracy can coexist with a large complete-chain deficit. The predeclared joint-encoding extension subsequently improved chain F1 over matched structured continuation and exceeded the fixed Helixer operating point in both species on the historically reused DEV panel, but remained below ANNEVO and Tiberius. Its pooled recall was lower than Helixer's, so the F1 advantage does not establish uniformly better gene recovery. A reference-informed compatibility oracle measures structural capacity rather than learned prediction accuracy, while matched score replay describes score competition without identifying a causal repair. The [Results](#results) reports the completed comparisons and these distinctions.

Sequence-only inference also does not imply exposure-free generalization. An annotation-pretrained encoder can retain upstream supervision even when its original classifier is removed, and a species absent from downstream head fitting may still have been present upstream. We therefore distinguish fitting diagnostics, reused development comparisons and genuinely independent evaluation, with exposure assessed separately for each model. The [exposure record](../reports/M28-METHOD-RESTART/pretrained_exposure.md) preserves the known and unresolved boundaries. The intended contribution is competitive complete-CDS-chain annotation or independently demonstrated applicability, supported by controlled mechanism evidence; neither the implementation nor the completed fitting and development comparisons establish that contribution on their own.

## Results

Joint 24,576-base encoding exceeded the fixed Helixer comparator in complete CDS chain F1 on both species in the original TRAIN panel, but remained below ANNEVO and Tiberius. Combining that encoding with the native CDS prior did not preserve its gain. The completed three-objective DEV comparison also retained a tradeoff: structured continuation had the highest pooled chain F1, while the mixed objective performed better in rice but worse in Arabidopsis and pooled accuracy, with more reference-relative background chains. TRAIN fitting and historically reused DEV measurements do not establish independent generalization or publication readiness. The [Methods](#methods) defines the model and inference procedure.

### Initial complete chain accuracy on the TRAIN panel

We evaluated the final model after 4,608 updates on six TRAIN chromosomes from Arabidopsis thaliana and Oryza sativa, totaling 156,779,632 bases. Training used 1,536 fixed draws from 1,491 unique windows, repeated for three passes, rather than exhaustive supervision of all chains on those chromosomes. The three native tools received the same six FASTA sequences. Inference used no reference annotations, training labels, RNA or protein evidence. Evaluation selected the longest eligible CDS per assessable reference gene, with transcript-ID tie-breaking, and retained 17,739 Arabidopsis and 4,949 rice primary chains. References outside the model grammar remained in the denominator; native predictions were not filtered to the grammar. An exact match required the correct sequence identifier, strand and every ordered CDS interval. CDS phase consistency was assessed separately.

The initial model recovered 9,966 of the 22,688 primary reference chains among 21,032 unique predicted chains. Pooled precision was 47.385%, recall was 43.926% and F1 was 45.590%. ANNEVO, Tiberius and Helixer attained pooled F1 values of 75.542%, 72.655% and 63.592%, respectively, exceeding the initial model by 29.952, 27.065 and 18.002 percentage points. Pooled scores were calculated from pooled counts, rather than averaging chromosome scores.

| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| Initial grammar model | 50.487 | 28.278 | 45.590 | 39.383 | 28.278 |
| ANNEVO | 76.831 | 71.179 | 75.542 | 74.005 | 71.179 |
| Tiberius | 75.417 | 63.577 | 72.655 | 69.497 | 63.577 |
| Helixer | 68.122 | 50.294 | 63.592 | 59.208 | 50.294 |

The deficit was present in both species and was larger in rice. The initial model's rice F1 was 28.278%, compared with 50.487% in Arabidopsis; every comparator exceeded it in both species. Thus, the failure to match these tools was already evident within the original training range and cannot be attributed solely to transfer beyond that range. This comparison does not isolate a particular training objective, representation or structural module as its cause. ### High CDS base accuracy did not imply accurate complete chains

The initial model achieved pooled CDS-base F1 of 90.360%, with base precision of 97.208% and recall of 84.414%, despite its substantially lower complete-chain F1. The corresponding CDS-base F1 values for ANNEVO, Tiberius and Helixer were 93.942%, 94.428% and 92.440%. This base metric measures the unstranded union of CDS intervals, whereas complete-chain matching requires all CDS boundaries and the strand to agree with one primary reference chain.

The initial model covered 0.618% of reference-relative background with predicted gene spans and produced 162 unique chains wholly within that background. The corresponding values were 1.190% and 383 for ANNEVO, 0.752% and 454 for Tiberius, and 3.613% and 1,614 for Helixer. Background was defined outside annotated gene-feature spans on either strand, not simply outside reference CDS intervals. Lower background burden and high base precision therefore did not establish complete-structure competitiveness.

All four methods had zero duplicate-chain records. Exact matches to other eligible reference isoforms, but not to the selected primary chain, numbered 748 for the initial model, 1,498 for ANNEVO, 1,552 for Tiberius and 1,339 for Helixer. These matches were reported separately rather than counted as primary true positives. A nonprimary match is not, by itself, evidence of a biologically false gene or a novel gene. These supplementary measures use the same [completed evaluation](../reports/M28-METHOD-RESTART/native_train_baseline_result.md).

### Completed continuation objectives on the TRAIN panel

We independently resumed the initial weights and saved AdamW state for a further 4,608 updates over three passes through the same ordered TRAIN draws. The backbone, labels, importance weights, optimizer settings and raw-FASTA caller remained fixed. The three branches retained the structured observation likelihood, replaced it with independent local role-observation likelihoods, or combined the structured and local negative log-likelihood terms with coefficients of 0.5 each. These coefficients do not equalize gradient magnitudes. We evaluated all three predeclared final weights at 9,216 updates, without intermediate-checkpoint selection, using the same six whole chromosomes and 22,688-reference evaluation denominator.

Structured continuation correctly matched 12,696 primary chains among 22,862 unique predictions. Pooled precision was 55.533%, recall was 55.959% and F1 was 55.745%, exceeding the initial model by 10.155 percentage points. Arabidopsis and rice chain F1 increased by 11.349 and 7.135 percentage points, respectively. Species macro F1 was 48.624%, with rice remaining the worst species at 35.413%. All three continuations remained below the three fixed native comparators in both species; even the highest pooled continuation score was 7.847 percentage points below Helixer.

At the same update count, structured continuation exceeded local continuation in pooled chain F1 by 14.0164 percentage points, despite a 0.8965-percentage-point lower CDS-base F1. It produced 197 wholly background chains, compared with 491 for local continuation. Local continuation increased rice chain F1 relative to the initial model but reduced Arabidopsis and pooled chain F1, despite improving pooled CDS-base F1. All three continuations retain the same complete-gene caller, so this is a contrast between training objectives rather than a decoder ablation. Matching update counts does not match gradient magnitudes or effective updates, and comparisons with the initial model also differ by 4,608 additional updates. ## Joint structural compatibility did not explain the large accuracy gap

We assessed whether the grammar could represent the reference chains jointly, rather than only one chain at a time. Of the 22,688 primary references, 22,479 satisfied the implemented DNA grammar individually. For each chromosome and strand, we then found the maximum number of supported reference CDS spans that could coexist without overlap. Touching half-open spans were compatible, and opposite strands were evaluated independently. The maximum compatible set contained 22,460 references: 209 references were individually unsupported, and joint compatibility imposed a further loss of 19 supported references.

| TRAIN scope | All references N | Maximum supported compatible references M | Minimum forced omissions | Reference oracle F1 % |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| Arabidopsis | 17,739 | 17,613 | 126 | 99.6436 |
| Rice | 4,949 | 4,847 | 102 | 98.9588 |
| Pooled | 22,688 | 22,460 | 228 | 99.4950 |

If an oracle outputs only these M reference chains and no additional predictions, its precision is one, recall is M/N and F1 is 2M/(N+M). The pooled value of 99.4950% is therefore a reference-informed structural ceiling within the declared grammar. It is not raw-FASTA model accuracy, a guarantee that the neural scores can realize the oracle, or evidence of independent generalization. A reference omitted from one maximum set may belong to another equally large set.

All 16,470 primary references correctly recovered by ANNEVO and all 16,095 recovered by Tiberius were individually grammar-supported and jointly compatible within each tool's own hit set. This statement does not combine the two tools' hit sets. Helixer recovered 15,091 primary references, of which 15,072 were grammar-supported and jointly compatible; its remaining 19 hits were outside the grammar and stayed in the original evaluation. These results show that the grammar and same-strand nonoverlap constraint alone cannot explain the initial model's large deficit relative to ANNEVO and Tiberius. They do not identify whether scoring, supervision, optimization or their interaction is responsible. ### Scope of the evidence

The [Discussion](#discussion) relates these complete-chain measurements and score diagnostics to the contribution under test, without treating them as a causal mechanism or independent validation.

These are single-fit diagnostics on the initial training range and historically reused same-species DEV chromosomes, not independent tests or a matched training-budget comparison with the native tools. Upstream pretraining and annotation exposure are model-dependent; freezing an annotation-pretrained backbone does not establish exposure-free prediction. The [exposure record](../reports/M28-METHOD-RESTART/pretrained_exposure.md) preserves these distinctions. The scope is complete protein-coding CDS chains, not UTR annotation or recovery of every alternative isoform.

The completed inference runs also do not establish a computational-efficiency advantage. CPU allocations, software environments, concurrent node workloads and timing boundaries differed between the initial model and the native wrappers; the [execution record](../reports/M28-METHOD-RESTART/native_train_baseline_result.md#完成运行的资源占用) reports the observed allocations without treating them as a fair speed benchmark. Competitive complete-chain performance, a controlled explanation of any improvement and independently supported broader applicability remain to be established by the corresponding experiments.

## Discussion

Complete CDS chain accuracy improved with joint neural encoding, but the completed TRAIN and reused DEV comparisons do not yet establish superiority to the strongest fixed comparators or independently broader applicability. The [Results](#results) support a distinction between the structures a grammar permits, the scores a model learns and the complete chains its caller recovers.

### Structure aware learning and the contribution under test

The implemented model connects partial observations to competition over complete, compatible gene sets, including the empty set. Fine observations restrict the latent roles, coarse observations contribute a fixed observation channel, and unknown positions are marginalized rather than labelled as background. Raw FASTA inference then selects a complete set under the same DNA grammar, using whole-contig scores assembled from finite neural windows. These definitions provide a specific learning problem, not a demonstration that the resulting predictor is more accurate. Training still uses finite-view likelihoods rather than a chromosome-joint likelihood; a whole-contig solve does not make the encoder's context chromosome-wide. The [Methods](#methods) define these distinctions.

Structure-aware neural prediction has direct precedents. Tiberius integrates a differentiable HMM into fine-tuning and inference, while GeneDecoder trains learned representations with a latent CRF and uses Viterbi prediction. Structured training, latent roles and a neural encoder followed by constrained decoding therefore cannot establish novelty on their own. The contribution under test is whether the specified treatment of partial observations and complete-set competition improves the final annotation task. The present comparisons do not establish a first-of-kind method or isolate the benefit of the observation channel. [Tiberius Methods](https://academic.oup.com/bioinformatics/article/40/12/btae685/7903281), [GeneDecoder Methods](https://arxiv.org/html/2505.03377v1).

The fixed-head coarse-observation diagnostic illustrates why an implemented likelihood and a performance mechanism must be distinguished. The first coarse-bearing TRAIN views, containing only one and three coarse rows, had no additional returned logit derivative; maximum-count views from the same unchanged schedule, containing 7,127 and 17,474 rows, did. Uniform-channel entropy constants and fine-only derivatives reconciled in all four views. This establishes that coarse information can affect a local learning signal at this head, not that it improves complete-chain prediction, is necessary across the population or caused the observed accuracy gains. The count-selected follow-up was specified after the first results were known. The [diagnostic and its boundaries](../reports/M28-METHOD-RESTART/full_grammar_coarse_information_probe.md) motivate a matched additional-learning contrast rather than a new claim of mechanism validation.

The completed additional-learning contrast further limits this mechanism claim. Retaining coarse information rather than a uniform channel lowered Arabidopsis chain F1 by 0.908 points and pooled F1 by 0.342 points, while raising rice F1 by 1.537 points. This single-fit contrast starts after 4,608 informative updates; it neither establishes full-training necessity nor justifies deleting the channel or selecting a TRAIN winner for DEV. The [completed result](../reports/M28-METHOD-RESTART/full_grammar_uniform_coarse_result.md) retains the tradeoff. On the historically reused DEV panel, informative minus uniform F1 was +0.312 points pooled, −0.459 in Arabidopsis and +2.084 in rice. The different pooled sign and persistent species tradeoff reinforce the distinction between an active learning signal and a universal accuracy mechanism.

### Complete chain performance and comparator dependence

The initial model's pooled TRAIN CDS-base F1 of 90.360% coexisted with complete-chain F1 of 45.590%. Complete structures require all coding intervals and the strand to match one primary reference, whereas our base endpoint measures the unstranded union of CDS intervals. Neither good coding coverage nor low reference-relative background burden substitutes for this structural endpoint. The matched structured-versus-local continuation contrast reinforces that distinction: the structured objective had higher complete-chain F1 despite lower CDS-base F1, with the same final caller. This is an objective comparison, not evidence that the caller is necessary or that one module caused the entire improvement.

Joint encoding attained pooled chain F1 of 66.963% on TRAIN and 66.623% on the reused DEV chromosomes. Its DEV F1 exceeded the fixed Helixer comparator in both species, but the pooled advantage combined 7.806-percentage-point higher precision with 0.543-point lower recall. It recovered 42 fewer primary chains than Helixer overall, including 134 fewer in rice. It also remained 5.182 points below Tiberius and 9.080 below ANNEVO pooled. Thus the observed gain is meaningful for this complete-chain operating point without demonstrating universal superiority, uniformly greater recovery or a new state of the art. The [fixed DEV comparison](../reports/M28-METHOD-RESTART/full_grammar_sampling_context_dev_plan.md#十方法扩展-dev-终态与结果) retains every method and the unchanged reference denominator.

Native Carbon-A provides both a complete-structure precedent and a completed original-TRAIN pipeline comparison. Its fixed filtered and unfiltered outputs reached pooled chain F1 of 78.172% and 76.063%, respectively, compared with 66.963% for the completed joint-encoding candidate. The current candidate therefore remains below this strong complete-pipeline comparator. The fixed confidence filter increased F1 while losing 127 primary hits, emphasizing the distinction between endpoint balance and greater recovery. Carbon-A's preserved main weights share the annotation-pretrained source used here, but its context, native CDS heads, learned transition and confidence networks, grammar and filtering differ. Shared main weights therefore do not isolate a decoder effect. Unfiltered predictions still depend on the learned transition network, and each auxiliary network's training and validation exposure must be considered separately from the main-model assembly list. This TRAIN-only comparison is not part of the frozen DEV family and does not establish independent generalization, clean zero-shot prediction or matched computational efficiency. The [completed comparison](../reports/M28-METHOD-RESTART/carbon_a_original_train_result.md) and [component-specific exposure record](../reports/M28-METHOD-RESTART/pretrained_exposure.md) define these boundaries.

The paired TRAIN diagnostic concentrates the current deficit in multisegment references: they contributed 95.279% of filtered Carbon-A's net additional primary hits, while only one net hit came from a reference spanning more than the 24,576-base joint window. This makes recovery of complete multisegment structures a concrete endpoint for interpreting the ongoing original-scope fits; it does not establish that context is irrelevant or that any particular scoring component causes the gap. The 590 filtered-Carbon-A misses recovered by joint encoding show nonidentical reference-hit sets, not a deployable ensemble or newly discovered genes. Both fixed native operating points remain reported without threshold selection.

The native window-policy intervention offers a narrower test of the context hypothesis. Reducing Carbon-A's window to 24,576 bases lowered filtered and unfiltered pooled F1 by 0.609 and 0.478 percentage points, respectively, with declines on every chromosome, but left gaps of 10.600 and 8.622 points over the completed joint candidate. The tested window-policy change therefore did not remove most of the observed pipeline separation. Because absolute overlap was fixed, relative overlap, window placement and averaging geometry changed together; these differences cannot be attributed to context length alone. Neither a causal fraction of the cross-pipeline gap nor the outcome of extending the grammar model's window can be inferred from this [TRAIN-only result](../reports/M28-METHOD-RESTART/carbon_a_context24_train_result.md).

The primary-chain endpoint also needs to be distinguished from annotation support more generally. The default filtered native output included 1,365 exact matches to other assessable isoforms that remained nonprimary benchmark residuals; the completed joint output included 1,267. Such matches are annotation-relative evidence for CDS geometry, not independent biological validation. Conversely, matching neither exact reference category does not prove a predicted gene false. These global unique-prediction counts cannot explain which primary references were lost or establish same-gene alternative recovery, and they do not change the fixed comparison or justify post hoc endpoint selection.

The completed sampling-by-encoding contrast strengthens the within-panel result without closing the competitive gap. Fresh-joint reached pooled TRAIN chain F1 of 70.303%, improving over original-joint by 3.340 points and exceeding Helixer in both species, but still falling below Tiberius, ANNEVO and the native Carbon-A operating points. The pooled interaction was −3.988 points on the nonlinear F1 scale, so the two observed improvements should not be described as superadditive complementarity. A decrease in CDS-base F1 and an increase in background burden also caution against equating higher exact-chain F1 with uniformly cleaner annotations. The separate frozen DEV comparison subsequently reached 70.107% pooled F1 for fresh-joint, exceeding Helixer in both species but remaining below Tiberius and ANNEVO. This historically reused development result narrows the observed gap without establishing independently broader applicability.

Low-rank encoder adaptation further narrowed the original-TRAIN gap: pooled complete-chain F1 reached 72.958%, 2.656 points above fresh-joint, with gains in both species. It slightly exceeded Tiberius pooled while remaining lower in Arabidopsis, and still fell below ANNEVO and the default Carbon-A pipelines. Increased background burden and lower CDS-base precision temper the chain-recovery gain. This supports a conditional performance benefit of the specified adaptation, not an isolated capacity or optimization-age mechanism, uniform state-of-the-art performance or independent generalization. The [completed adaptation result](../reports/M28-METHOD-RESTART/full_grammar_joint_lora_result.md) stays TRAIN-only and does not enter the frozen DEV family.

The completed full-rank q/v continuation also improved the frozen fresh-joint TRAIN control on every chromosome, reaching pooled F1 of 72.200%, but remained below Tiberius pooled and ANNEVO in both species. It was 0.758 points below the rank-four intervention pooled, while having lower background burden and slightly higher CDS-base F1. The two fixed settings therefore exhibit different endpoint balances; they do not isolate the causal effect of rank, because learning rate, parameterization, forward rounding and optimization trajectories also differ.

The four-cell native-learning-rate contrast makes that conditional ordering explicit. Raising the native rate from 1e-5 to 3e-4 increased rank-four pooled chain F1 from 65.929% to 72.958%, but reduced full-rank F1 from 72.200% to 58.982%. Full rank exceeded rank four at the lower matched rate; rank four exceeded full rank at the higher rate, with the same reversal in both species. Neither newly completed cell improved its parameterization's original setting. The head learning rate, starting head weights and moments, ordered draws, observation likelihood and final update count were fixed. Nevertheless, native-rate matching does not match the parameterization, BF16 forward rounding, joint clipping or optimizer trajectory, and each cell has one fit/seed. The result therefore constrains a rank-only interpretation without establishing its cause, a new validation winner or a transferable learning-rate rule. The [complete four-cell result](../reports/M28-METHOD-RESTART/full_grammar_qv_lr_controls_result.md) retains every cell and negative comparison.

Read-only reference replay further showed that the full-rank predictor was not simply a subset of the low-rank predictor: it retained 15,636 shared hits, gained 470 and lost 784. Most net losses occurred among multisegment references, but these post-outcome bins are descriptive recall accounting and do not establish why the structures were lost. Neither overlapping reference-hit sets nor lower background coverage provide a reference-free ensemble rule or independent biological validation. The [completed full-rank record](../reports/M28-METHOD-RESTART/full_grammar_dense_qv_result.md) preserves this tradeoff without extending DEV scope or selecting subsequent training.

In contrast, complete-spliced-CDS codon energies lowered pooled TRAIN chain F1 by 4.834 points, with declines on every chromosome, fewer primary hits and more predictions. Verified structural scoring and added parameters therefore did not guarantee improved recovery. The [completed negative contrast](../reports/M28-METHOD-RESTART/full_grammar_codon_result.md) changes the trained head and its optimization trajectory as well as adding content energies, so it cannot alone attribute the loss to individual codon coefficients or disprove coding-content modeling generally. It was retained in the frozen DEV design, where its pooled F1 was 5.528 points below fresh-joint, with declines in both species. Agreement across these fitting and reused-development views does not make the intervention a clean causal isolation or an independent test. The [complete fixed-family result](../reports/M28-METHOD-RESTART/full_grammar_joint_controls_dev_result.md) preserves the negative branch.

### Structural capacity and learned score competition

The reference-informed TRAIN compatibility oracle could retain 22,460 of 22,688 primary chains within the implemented grammar and same-strand nonoverlap constraint. Its 99.4950% F1 ceiling assumes access to the references and no additional predictions; it is not achievable model accuracy established by raw FASTA. Nevertheless, the 209 individually unsupported references and 19 further compatibility losses cannot, by themselves, explain the much larger learned accuracy deficit. Grammar capacity and learning success are separate questions.

The matched final-update score replay narrows the descriptive account of that deficit. Of joint encoding's 7,762 missed TRAIN references, 2,486 had negative energy, 5,067 had positive energy but were outscored by their complete selected conflict sets, and 209 were individually unsupported. Of the 2,956 references newly recovered relative to structured continuation, 2,123 had previously been positive but outscored; 833 had been negative. Another 1,085 changed from negative to positive-but-outscored without recovery. These migrations show why a reduction in the negative-energy count alone cannot quantify recovered genes. No legal single-reference replacement strictly improved either frozen selected set, which supports scoring consistency rather than independently proving global MAP optimality. The [matched replay](../reports/M28-METHOD-RESTART/full_grammar_matched_energy_diagnostic.md#完整终态与评分结果) preserves the complete paired population.

When gained chains were scored with the old structured head and lost or jointly missed supported chains with the joint head, intron terms were the largest negative mean contribution. This is an arithmetic feature of unnormalized reference-minus-conflict score sums, not a causal diagnosis of intron modeling. Chain length, role exposure and conflict geometry differ, the groups are defined by prediction outcomes, and absolute score scales across trained heads are not calibrated. An exact selected chain's zero replacement margin is a self-comparison, not an explanation of recovery. Consequently, the replay does not justify changing intron weights or claim that such a change would repair missed chains.

### Sampling and additional evidence are not additive guarantees

Neither more complete-core supervision nor the combination of native CDS priors with joint encoding improved reference recovery uniformly. The complete-core proposal increased directly observed complete-chain coverage but lost net hits relative to fresh uniform sampling, while the prior-and-context combination had lower complete-chain F1 than either component alone. Importance correction matches an expected objective, not finite exposure, clipped gradients or AdamW trajectories. Likewise, matching update counts does not match effective optimization or upstream annotation exposure. The existing four-cell prior-and-context contrast is descriptive and does not establish mechanistic antagonism. The completed fresh-sampling-by-joint-encoding comparison also had a negative interaction on the nonlinear F1 scale. None of these single-fit results establishes general causal antagonism or expands the frozen DEV family.

### Biological scope and independent evidence

The model currently annotates complete protein-coding CDS chains, not UTRs, noncoding genes or every alternative isoform. All unsupported references remain in evaluation, and all seven earlier M28 weights and all three new fixed candidates missed the three DEV references spanning more than a native read window. Whole-contig decoding alone therefore establishes neither long-gene recovery nor broader feature coverage. Similarly, single-fit measurements from two species and historically reused DEV chromosomes cannot establish broad taxonomic applicability or clean zero-shot generalization. The annotation-pretrained backbone retains upstream supervision even when frozen and separated from its original classifier; exposure isolation remains model-dependent. The [exposure record](../reports/M28-METHOD-RESTART/pretrained_exposure.md) identifies the known and unresolved boundaries.

A stronger performance or applicability claim requires independent evaluation with explicit sequence and homology isolation, relevant comparator versions and complete-chain denominators, together with controlled evidence for the proposed mechanism. Repeatability and matched computational measurements would address different questions from accuracy; the current execution timings do not establish an efficiency advantage. The present results motivate continued investigation of complete-set scoring under partial observations, but do not yet satisfy those broader claims.

## Methods

The [model schematic](figures/M28_full_grammar_model.svg) separates frozen feature extraction, training-only observations and whole-contig raw FASTA decoding. Its [caption](figures/M28_full_grammar_model_caption.md) states the base-model and structural scope.

### Task and representation

The model predicts complete protein-coding CDS exon chains from genomic FASTA. It does not predict UTRs or enumerate all alternative isoforms. Each orientation is processed separately, with nonoverlapping gene spans within that orientation; overlapping genes on opposite strands remain possible. FASTA sequences are uppercased and non-ACGT symbols are mapped to N without changing sequence length or coordinates. Internal genomic intervals are zero-based and half-open.

The base model uses the frozen GENERanno-eukaryote-1.2b-cds-annotator-preview checkpoint at revision b0483c23b6b63787b61a6d3a204a9b517d6ba345. A 24,576-base neural window is encoded as four native blocks of at most 6,144 bases. Aligned six-mer representations have 2,048 channels; BF16 encoder outputs are materialized as FP32 inputs to the trainable head. This is an annotation-pretrained checkpoint, not an audited exposure-free unsupervised backbone. Freezing it or omitting its original classifier does not remove upstream annotation exposure. The [asset exposure record](../reports/M28-METHOD-RESTART/pretrained_exposure.md) separates disclosed exposure from unresolved training-window and homology isolation.

Carbon-A's fixed provenance maps its preserved main weights to this revision. Its report places both project assemblies in the training-snapshot reference subset, not the temporal holdout. Disclosed annotation supervision and assembly membership do not identify optimizer windows or establish validation and homology isolation. Strict raw-FASTA inference specifies prediction inputs, not assembly-unseen, species-unseen or homology-isolated evaluation. [Fixed provenance](https://huggingface.co/HuggingFaceBio/Carbon-A-1.2B/raw/f6789ee79e6ef397de333577ffec1cb734496620/provenance.json), [report sections 5.1–5.2 and Table S3](https://huggingface.co/buckets/HuggingFaceBio/Carbon-A-Technical-Report/resolve/Carbon-A.pdf).

The base head has 366,191 FP32 parameters. It projects token representations to 128 dimensions and uses a bidirectional GRU with 64 units in each direction. Packed lengths exclude padded tokens. Token outputs are repeated over their six aligned bases and concatenated with a 32-channel DNA branch comprising two convolutions, with kernel sizes nine and five and GELU activations. A linear layer, GELU and layer normalization fuse the branches. A final linear layer emits fifteen unnormalized role scores per valid base. The roles distinguish background, coding phase, intronic phase, donor and acceptor boundary roles, start and stop. Only the head is optimized; the backbone remains frozen. These definitions follow the actual C0 implementation in `src/m28/core.py` and the [first-fit recipe](../reports/M28-METHOD-RESTART/full_grammar_first_fit_contract.md).

### Complete structures and score

Let S(x) be the complete, same-strand, span-compatible gene sets accepted by the implemented DNA automaton for an oriented sequence x. The empty set is included. A gene begins with ATG and terminates with TAA, TAG or TGA within one exon. The automaton retains the actual unfinished coding bases across introns, excludes internal in-frame stops, accepts GT or GC donors followed by AG acceptors, requires at least five intronic bases and permits at most 128 exons per gene. Ambiguous bases cannot complete a coding codon, start or splice literal; they may occur in background or intronic interior. Nonstandard structures outside this universe are limitations of the model, not grounds for removing evaluation references.

For a gene set S, let rS(b) be its role at base b, with background role zero, and let zθ(b,r;x) be the head score. Its energy is the unit-weight sum `Eθ(S;x) = Σb [zθ(b,rS(b);x) − zθ(b,0;x)]`. The empty-set energy is zero. There is no learned null offset, length normalization, candidate-pool score or fitted inference threshold. Actual codon carry and the fixed exon budget belong to the structural state, rather than being inferred from a remainder-only phase label.

All-path training and max-path inference use this same structural universe, but different semirings. The training denominator sums the mass of every permitted complete set; it does not restrict competition to an enumerated proposal pool or a Viterbi winner. Exactness is relative to this explicit grammar and supplied sequence, not to all biologically possible genes. The transitions are defined in `src/m28/full_grammar_boundary_messages.py`.

### Partial observations and training likelihood

Only the original core positions carry training observations. A resolved fine label contributes the indicator that the latent role equals that label. When fine is unknown but a coarse label is known, the observation channel assigns probability 0.9 to a matching region class and 0.05 to each of the other two classes. The classes are background, coding including exonic boundary/start/stop roles, and intronic roles. Fully unknown positions and all positions outside the core contribute one. Fine takes precedence over coarse. The fixed channel parameter ε = 0.1 is neither optimized nor interpreted as an estimated biological annotation error rate.

Define `Q(o|S) = ∏b q_b(o_b|rS(b))`, `Zall = ΣS∈S(x) exp Eθ(S;x)` and `Zobs = ΣS∈S(x) exp Eθ(S;x) Q(o|S)`. The unnormalized observation loss is `Lobs = log Zall − log Zobs`. Unknown positions are marginalized rather than filled with background or a selected reference chain. Soft coarse observations do not eliminate the need for a feasible exact-fine event.

The implementation enforces exact fine observations through an allowed-role mask and adds log-likelihood factors only at coarse-only core positions. Because its energy subtracts the background logit, it restores `Σb∈C log q_b(o_b|0)` after that relative partition calculation, where C is the set of coarse-only core positions. Exact-fine indicators are not part of this background correction. Omitting the correction would change the absolute observation probability. The observation formula is implemented in `src/m28/full_grammar_coarse_channel.py`; original ambiguity and callable-base masks are retained rather than relabelled to increase support.

A supplemental TRAIN label assay joined the unchanged 22,688 primary references to the existing 25,512-window pool. Region concordance was evaluated only for individually supported references fully contained in an existing core; unsupported and geometry-ineligible references remained separately unassessed. Original fine, coarse and unknown counts were replayed for every saved reference-core pair. At original coarse-only rows, four in-memory target constructions retained the original masks, cleared only explicit fine-unknown intervals, removed only alternative transcripts from genes with a primary, or applied both changes. Alternative-only gene groups, DNA and callable masks remained protected. All eight fine-restoration patterns were retained. These diagnostic targets were never used for training or inference, and concordance with the same source annotation does not establish independent biological truth or whole-window observation feasibility. The [coarse-label and mask assay](../reports/M28-METHOD-RESTART/full_grammar_coarse_mask_census.md) defines the contrasts and accounting.

Training views contain the original 24,576-base core and its raw-coordinate stride-neighbor windows on either side, clipped and deduplicated at chromosome boundaries. A base receives all fifteen scores from its nearest containing window center; ties choose the lower genomic start on both strands. Reverse-complement processing changes row coordinates and piece order, not role columns. Interior message boundaries carry all reachable scored states; only the outer edges of the finite DNA view are closed in background. Consequently, this is a collection of finite-view observation likelihoods, not chromosome-joint maximum likelihood, even though inference later decodes whole contigs.

FP64 log-space sum-product messages retain every reachable state. A checkpointed CPU implementation recomputes blocks during reverse-mode differentiation and supplies first-order derivatives through incoming messages and neural logits. Its 256-base checkpoint spacing changes memory use, not structural closure. Exon-budget dominance used by the max decoder is not applied to these partitions: discarding a lower-probability reachable path would change their summed mass. The implementation is `src/m28/full_grammar_boundary_compiled.py`.

### Optimization and sampling

For each draw w, the fitted objective is `a_w Lobs(w) / max(1, F_w + C_w)`, where F_w and C_w count fine and coarse-only core rows and a_w is the specified importance weight. The denominator is known observation rows, not all valid bases, gene count or chain length. A fully unknown draw remains in the update sequence with zero observation loss; AdamW momentum and weight decay can still change parameters.

The first fit used a fresh seed-zero head, 1,536 fixed draws from 1,491 unique windows, three repeated passes and 4,608 updates. AdamW used learning rate 3×10⁻⁴, weight decay 0.01, betas 0.9/0.999, epsilon 10⁻⁸ and gradient-norm clipping at one, with batch size one and no scheduler. Native frozen representations were recomputed per draw; feature caches and old supervised head weights were not model inputs. The formal readout uses the last checkpoint, not the best intermediate loss or DEV score. Continuations restore that checkpoint and its AdamW state for another fixed 4,608 updates; differing objectives, context and native-head-prior branches retain their separate protocols rather than being described as one controlled factor.

For the objective contrast, local role probabilities are `pθ(b,r) = exp zθ(b,r;x) / Σk exp zθ(b,k;x)`, with the sum over all fifteen roles, and the unnormalized local observation loss is `Llocal = −Σb∈core log[Σr pθ(b,r) q_b(o_b|r)]`. The observation factors use the same fine/coarse hierarchy as the structured likelihood: a fine label selects its exact role probability, while a coarse-only label weights matching-region roles by 0.9 and other roles by 0.05 before summing their probabilities. This is a fifteen-role softmax, not a separately normalized three-region classifier. Fully unknown core rows have unit observation likelihood and zero local gradient. The local calculation uses FP64 log-softmax and log-sum-exp, without enforcing literal DNA or complete-chain compatibility in its training likelihood.

The predeclared structured, local and mixed continuation losses are respectively `a_w Lobs / d_w`, `a_w Llocal / d_w` and `a_w (Lobs + Llocal) / (2 d_w)`, where `d_w = max(1, F_w + C_w)`. The original draws, order, observations, importance weights, optimizer settings and starting AdamW moments are unchanged, with the formal readout fixed at cumulative update 9,216. No additional class, boundary, length or null weights are introduced. The mixed coefficients are fixed at 0.5 and do not equalize loss or gradient scales; matching update counts does not establish matched effective update magnitudes. All three readouts retain the same complete-gene grammar and whole-contig raw-FASTA caller, even when the training objective is local. These definitions follow `src/m28/local_coarse_channel.py` and the [objective contrast protocol](../reports/M28-METHOD-RESTART/full_grammar_objective_contrast_contract.md). The completed objective comparison uses these three final readouts without DEV-based selection.

The complete-core sampling branch uses the existing natural TRAIN windows, stratified by species, chromosome and orientation. In a stratum with N windows, k_w is the number of supported primary-reference incidences wholly contained in w with every span role resolved and matching fine supervision, and K = Σw k_w. Its fixed proposal is `q_w = 0.5/N + 0.5 k_w/K`, with replacement, and `a_w = 1/(N q_w)`. All declared strata have K > 0. Every natural and background window retains positive probability, and a_w is at most two. This preserves the within-stratum uniform expectation of the normalized per-window loss before nonlinear optimizer operations; it does not match clipping effects, variance or AdamW trajectories. Eligibility depends on TRAIN geometry and labels, never baseline or model predictions. The [complete-core protocol](../reports/M28-METHOD-RESTART/full_grammar_complete_core_draws_plan.md) fixes the sampling seed and schedule and compares the final 9,216-update model with both fresh-uniform and repeated-structured controls at the same update count.

### Native CDS priors and joint-window encoding

The native-prior branch retained the original head and added a zero-initialized, bias-free linear map from two native CDS-evidence columns to the fourteen nonbackground role increments. For each valid base, each evidence column was the frozen native head's six-position CDS score minus its NON_CODING score, materialized in FP32. If u_h(b) denotes that evidence, the modified role score is `z′(b,r) = z(b,r) + Σh λ[r,h] u_h(b)` for r = 1,…,14; role zero is unchanged. The 28 coefficients were trainable, but both original BF16 classifiers and the encoder remained frozen. The branch had 366,219 trainable parameters, with no auxiliary classifier loss, fitted inference threshold or native-head selection. Native annotation-pretrained scores are reused inputs, not a new model contribution by themselves.

The native-prior continuation restored all original head AdamW moments at step 4,608 and appended a new coefficient group starting at step zero. All parameters used the same optimizer settings and one common gradient-norm clip; after 4,608 additional updates, original parameters reached step 9,216 and new coefficients step 4,608. Original draws, importance, observations and structured likelihood were unchanged. Parameter addition changes gradient flow and shared clipping, so matched schedules do not imply identical effective optimization. The [native-prior protocol](../reports/M28-METHOD-RESTART/full_grammar_native_cds_prior_fit.md) fixes the coefficient and checkpoint identities.

The joint-context branch replaced four separate 6,144-base encoder blocks with one native forward over the same 24,576-base read window, corresponding to 4,096 six-base tokens. It retained the fixed GENERanno revision, bidirectional SDPA flash attention, BF16 hidden states and FP32 head inputs, without changing backbone parameters or rotary embeddings. Short windows were oriented first and right-padded with N; only valid original bases entered the score field and grammar. This branch did not use the native CDS classifiers. The combined branch used this encoding and the same two-head prior map. Both retained the original draws, additional update count and whole-contig decoder; the [joint](../reports/M28-METHOD-RESTART/full_grammar_joint24_fit.md) and [combined](../reports/M28-METHOD-RESTART/full_grammar_joint24_native_prior_plan.md) protocols specify their independent final checkpoints.

The fresh-uniform branch instead used a fixed seed-one schedule of 4,608 draws from 4,219 unique original natural TRAIN windows, with importance one. It kept the original 6,144-base encoding, structured observation loss and optimizer starting state. The complete-core mixture described above used 4,107 unique windows. These sampling contrasts change finite supervision exposure and gradient variation, not just the number of fully covered reference chains. All branches used their prespecified final update-9,216 readout, rather than a checkpoint chosen from TRAIN or DEV performance.

The fixed TRAIN interaction is F11−F10−F01+F00 on count-derived complete-chain endpoints, separately for chromosomes, species, pooled counts, species macro and worst species. ### Complete CDS codon energies

The codon branch augments the role-only energy without changing the accepted structures. Let n_k(S;x) count codon k in the oriented, spliced CDS sequences of S. The 64 raw FP32 coefficients β follow lexicographic ACGT order. For each codon, `β̄_k = β_k − mean_{j∈G(k)} β_j`, where G(k) is either the 61 sense codons or the three stops. Equivalently, β̄ = Pβ for the block-diagonal within-group centering projection P. The augmented energy is `Eθ,β(S;x) = Eθ(S;x) + Σk n_k(S;x) β̄_k`. The empty set retains energy zero, and β = 0 exactly nests the role-only model. Centering leaves 62 coefficient directions; it does not establish that all of them are independently identifiable from the jointly fitted neural scores.

Every complete CDS triplet contributes once, including the initial ATG and the terminal TAA, TAG or TGA. Internal triplets may span introns: the unchanged automaton carries their actual unfinished coding bases and scores a triplet only when its third coding base is consumed. Intronic and background bases receive no codon contribution. Stops split across introns, internal in-frame stops, ambiguous coding bases and incomplete genes remain excluded. The implementation adds codon indices to existing closure edges rather than introducing states or paths; training partitions, MAP decoding and reported chain energies use the same coefficients.

Both Zall and Zobs replace Eθ with Eθ,β while retaining the original fine/coarse observation factors, absolute background correction and known-row normalization. Define `pall(S) = exp Eθ,β(S;x)/Zall` and `pobs(S) = exp Eθ,β(S;x) Q(o|S)/Zobs`. At fixed θ, the unnormalized observation-loss derivative is `∇β Lobs = Pᵀ [Epall n(S;x) − Epobs n(S;x)]`. The fitted per-draw objective multiplies this derivative by `a_w / max(1, F_w + C_w)`. Checkpointed FP64 sum-product differentiation accumulates the codon contributions through all reachable states and through incoming messages; it does not apply MAP dominance pruning. The actual-carry max-only skyline remains valid because dominated states share all future role and codon increments. The implementations are `src/m28/full_grammar_codon.py` and `src/m28/full_grammar_codon_skyline.py`.

The branch starts from the same update-4,608 head and restored AdamW state as fresh-sampling joint encoding. Its 64 coefficients start at zero with new optimizer moments. The unchanged seed-one schedule has 4,608 draws from 4,219 original TRAIN windows, importance one, joint 24,576-base encoding and ε = 0.1. Head and codon parameters share the original AdamW settings and one norm-one gradient clip; the encoder remains frozen and native CDS classifiers are not inputs. The sole formal readout has head optimizer age 9,216 and codon optimizer age 4,608, with 366,255 trainable parameters in total. Adding the coefficients also changes gradient flow and joint clipping, so the paired contrast tests the complete scoring-and-optimization intervention, not an isolated effect of codon composition. The [codon protocol](../reports/M28-METHOD-RESTART/full_grammar_codon_factor_preparation.md) specifies the strict final-checkpoint adapter and original six-chromosome TRAIN comparison. Its artificial-DNA and disposable native checks are implementation evidence, not final-model accuracy.

The coefficients are conditional energy terms, not codon probabilities or empirical codon-usage estimates. Group centering makes the energy invariant to a uniform raw-coefficient shift within either group; it does not make the sum neutral to observed composition, CDS length or gene count. In particular, every gene contributes an ATG start term in addition to any internal ATG terms, and one terminal stop term. Neural and codon energies must therefore be interpreted jointly, with complete-chain recovery and background burden assessed in the fixed paired readout rather than inferred from individual coefficient signs.

### Additional information and encoder adaptation contrasts

Uniform-coarse continuation tests the observation channel during additional learning, not its removal from training since initialization. It restores the same update-4,608 head and AdamW state as fresh-sampling joint encoding and retains the same seed-one order of 4,608 draws from 4,219 original TRAIN windows, joint 24,576-base encoding, importance one and original fine-plus-coarse denominator. Only the additional-fit channel changes from ε = 0.1 to ε = 2/3. Each coarse region then has probability one third for every latent role, so its raw negative log likelihood contributes the parameter-independent constant `C_w log 3`. Fine observations remain hard constraints and unknown positions remain marginalized; coarse targets are not deleted and the denominator is not replaced by a fine-only count. The original head's first 4,608 updates already used informative coarse observations, and upstream annotation exposure is unchanged. The final readout is cumulative update 9,216. The [uniform-coarse protocol](../reports/M28-METHOD-RESTART/full_grammar_uniform_coarse_continuation_plan.md) therefore tests the marginal effect of information in the additional learning phase, not a from-scratch coarse-free model or a universal necessity claim; absolute losses with different observation constants are not compared as accuracy measures.

A separate low-rank encoder-adaptation branch uses the same starting head, ordered draws, observations, joint context and complete-set objective as the fresh-sampling joint control. It attaches rank-four, scale-one residuals to the query and value projections of zero-based encoder layers 22–25, keeping every original encoder weight frozen. For each original BF16 projection output, the residual is computed as `B(A(x_FP32))` in FP32, rounded to BF16 and added without merging into the released weight. Each A tensor has shape 4×2,048; B has shape 2,048×4 for query and 256×4 for value. A is initialized uniformly within ±1/√2,048 using a local CPU generator with seed zero, and B starts at zero. The eight projections contain sixteen FP32 tensors and 102,400 new parameters; together with the original 366,191-parameter head, 468,591 parameters are trained. Encoding retains the native PyTorch SDPA FlashAttention backend and BF16-to-FP32 hidden representation. The encoder stays in evaluation mode without dropout or gradient checkpointing, but gradients pass through the adapters; evaluation mode does not make the adapted representation frozen.

Head AdamW moments continue from age 4,608 to 9,216, while adapter moments start at zero and reach 4,608. All trained parameters share the original optimizer settings and one norm-one gradient clip. The unchanged grammar, empty-set energy, observation channel and raw-FASTA caller receive no extra classifier loss, codon term or fitted threshold. Final checkpoints retain the head, all sixteen named adapters and optimizer state, not another copy of the pretrained backbone; inference restores the fixed base and all adapters before disabling gradients. The [adapter protocol](../reports/M28-METHOD-RESTART/full_grammar_joint_lora_fit.md) fixes the final readout and original six-chromosome comparison. Added capacity, different optimizer ages and resulting joint clipping and optimization trajectories are part of this intervention, not an isolated biological causal effect. This branch remains TRAIN-only and is not added to the separately prepared three-candidate DEV plan or the completed eight- and ten-method families. Disposable implementation and native preflight updates do not establish accuracy; both the coarse-channel and low-rank adaptation TRAIN contrasts have completed. The [completed adaptation result](../reports/M28-METHOD-RESTART/full_grammar_joint_lora_result.md) preserves the improved chain endpoints and increased background burden without changing the frozen DEV family. The [completed coarse-channel result](../reports/M28-METHOD-RESTART/full_grammar_uniform_coarse_result.md) does not change this protocol or select a DEV candidate.

The separately completed post-outcome adaptation replay used the saved 22,688-primary-reference profile and the two unchanged final-output GFFs, without rereading DNA, reference GFFs or checkpoints. Unique keys included species, contig, strand and ordered zero-based half-open CDS intervals; source evaluations had already checked phase and DNA. Reparsed TP, prediction and duplicate counts had to reproduce each completed chromosome evaluation. ## Raw FASTA decoding

Inference receives only FASTA and the fixed final checkpoint. It does not read annotations, observation-channel targets, feature caches or an injected reference candidate pool. Neural windows use the fixed 24,576/12,288-base width/stride grid, including terminal coverage. The same nearest-containing-center rule produces one fifteen-role row at every oriented contig base, without averaging overlapping predictions. A single complete-set MAP solve is then run for each entire oriented contig, allowing genes to cross neural-window boundaries. Genes are not closed inside individual windows and subsequently filtered by ownership.

The production decoder uses an exact max-only exon-budget skyline. At the same sequence position, stage and actual coding carry, a state with no lower score and fewer used exons dominates a higher-budget state because it retains all of the latter's possible futures. FP64 accumulation and 8,192-base traceback checkpoints retain the score and complete structure. Equal-score choices follow background continuation and the implementation's state-table arrival/lower-budget policy; exact optimal score does not imply identical tied chain identities across different backends. Genomic coordinates and CDS phases are exported as gene, mRNA and CDS GFF3 records and checked against the actual DNA. Implementation and completed input/output verification are linked in the [raw-FASTA contract](../reports/M28-METHOD-RESTART/full_grammar_raw_train_contract.md).

The separate equal-energy caller independently restores the rank-four and full-rank encoders from their fixed final checkpoints and freshly encodes the same input FASTA with each. It promotes their aligned fifteen-role rows to FP64 before averaging with fixed weights 0.5 and 0.5, then applies the same whole-contig MAP decoder. Its runtime reads neither cached arrays nor old predictions, references or labels, and role arrays are not persisted. Two fixed TRAIN views and a short artificial sequence were checked against independent branch reencoding, an independent FP64 averaging expression, full-chain geometry, exported gains and ordered CDS phases. The [native qualification](../reports/M28-METHOD-RESTART/full_grammar_e_m_raw_native.md) establishes this finite-input equivalence without substituting its timings or accuracy for whole-chromosome execution.

The minimum-centered caller separately implements a fixed local disagreement penalty. For each aligned base and role it computes the lower of the two FP64 energies relative to each branch's own background, equivalent to their centered mean minus half their absolute disagreement. This is not the minimum of whole-gene energies or an intersection of emitted chains. It restores the two native models independently and uses the same raw input normalization, context ownership, whole-contig decoder and GFF export; runtime inference does not read saved role arrays or references. The [completed native qualification](../reports/M28-METHOD-RESTART/full_grammar_minimum_centered_raw.md) reused the same two fixed TRAIN views and short sequence as the mean caller. All six oriented outputs matched independent fresh branch reencoding, an independent minimum-energy expression, whole-contig MAP gains, exported geometry and ordered phases. The two nonempty complete chains and four empty outputs were retained, with peak allocated VRAM of 4.536 GiB. This finite-input integration does not substitute for new whole-chromosome native timings or accuracy.

### CDS event marginal support

For fixed oriented DNA x and its unchanged role scores, the normalized structural model is `Pθ(S|x) = exp Eθ(S;x) / Zall(x)`. The denominator includes every complete, same-strand, span-compatible gene set in the specified grammar and the empty set. The readout queries the original MAP CDS chains; these queries do not define a candidate universe or restrict the denominator. No training observation factor `Q(o|S)`, reference annotation, fitted temperature or correctness classifier enters this inference distribution.

For a complete queried CDS chain g occupying the half-open span [a,b), define `αB(a)` as the log mass of complete exterior sets ending in background at a and `βB(b)` as the log mass of complete exterior sets beginning in background at b. Same-strand span exclusion separates the legal exterior structures on either side of g, giving

`log Pθ(g ∈ S | x) = αB(a) + Eθ(g;x) + βB(b) − log Zall(x)`.

Only the gene event constrains its exterior boundary states. The full partition is not reset or closed at internal query points, so competing structures may cross these points when g is absent. For one fixed complete compatible set S0, `log Pθ(S = S0 | x) = Σg∈S0 Eθ(g;x) − log Zall(x)`; the empty-set log probability is `−log Zall(x)`. A single-gene marginal sums over compatible exterior genes and alternative sets. It is therefore not generally the sigmoid of its gain against background, and the product of gene marginals does not generally give the exact-set probability. The two orientations remain independent; no opposite-strand exclusion is introduced.

The implementation in `src/m28/full_grammar_gene_posterior.py` uses two FP64 sum-product scans over the supplied DNA and unchanged actual-codon-carry/exon-budget automaton. It retains rolling state messages and the queried background boundary scalars, without max-only skyline pruning. Whole-contig role arrays are still retained; sparse boundary storage is not a claim that total memory is independent of sequence length. Log probabilities remain available when their exponentials underflow. Forward and reverse total masses must agree, and supported event mass must not exceed the full partition beyond the declared numerical tolerance.

Independent complete-set enumeration and direct role-constrained numerators validated the implementation on artificial sequences and fixed native TRAIN views. This qualification is finite-view mathematical and engineering evidence, not a completed whole-chromosome calibration or performance result. The [qualification record](../reports/M28-METHOD-RESTART/full_grammar_gene_posterior_preflight.md) and [whole-TRAIN readout protocol](../reports/M28-METHOD-RESTART/full_grammar_gene_posterior_train_plan.md) distinguish these scopes. Conditional structured modelling and exact inference have established [semi-Markov CRF precedents](https://proceedings.neurips.cc/paper_files/paper/2004/hash/eb06b9db06012a7a4179b8f3cb5384d3-Abstract.html); deriving an event marginal is not, by itself, an originality claim.

The numerically stabilized message kernels in `src/m28/full_grammar_scaled_mass.py` recenter every 256 consumed bases. Each complete forward or backward state vector is shifted by its current finite background component, and the removed constants are accumulated with compensated addition and restored at every queried boundary and at the global endpoint. Open-gene states are retained across these cuts. This uniform change of representation neither closes internal boundaries nor changes role energies, legal paths, empty-set energy or the event-probability formula. The original forward/reverse mass tolerances and probability guards remain unchanged; no temperature or threshold is fitted.

Eight synthetic checks, including a million-base product of independent literal ORF choices, qualified this representation against enumeration, analytic masses and the established probability tests. A subsequent read-only whole-chromosome qualification on the rank-four Arabidopsis chromosome NC_003070.9 plus orientation obtained identical forward and reverse totals in the reported FP64 representation, 844406.735716842, while preserving all 3,467 original MAP-query gains and legal chains. The completed readout then covered all 24 model–chromosome–orientation combinations and replayed both fixed prediction pools before reference access. It reused the original role arrays, weights, queries and unchanged numerical tolerances. One separately qualified orientation was reused; the other 23 were computed in the readout. The separate missed-reference score assay was completed after reconciling four fully written chromosome blocks and recomputing two chromosomes with the original scientific calculation, preserving the failed attempt and its source provenance. The [numerical qualification and completed readout record](../reports/M28-METHOD-RESTART/full_grammar_gene_posterior_train_plan.md) preserves these distinct outcomes.

Ranking uses the complete fixed prediction pool separately under exact-primary and any-assessable-isoform labels. AP integrates the grouped precision–recall curve and AUROC handles equal scores jointly; every declared fraction (25%, 50%, 75%, 90% and 100%) retains complete ties. Prediction-pool positive recall is kept separate from recall against all original primary references. Fixed probability bins and reference-relative Brier scores describe the two benchmark events without treating structural inclusion as a calibrated probability of biological correctness. No temperature, threshold or operating point is fitted, and the existing MAP GFFs remain unchanged.


Structural inclusion is not the same event as exact agreement with an annotation or biological correctness. The fixed TRAIN diagnostic protocol specifies comparisons of log-marginal and internal-gain rankings for exact-primary and exact-any-assessable-isoform matches among the already emitted predictions. Exact score ties stay grouped; average precision and AUROC use this selected prediction pool, excluding unpredicted references. Primary-reference recall separately retains the full reference denominator. Retained fractions 0.25, 0.5, 0.75, 0.9 and 1.0 are predefined diagnostics, not selected operating points or new filtered GFF outputs. Reference-relative Brier values and ten fixed equal-width probability bins assess mismatch between structural support and these reference events; they do not fit a calibrator, prove biological calibration or identify every unmatched prediction as a false gene. No frozen DEV family is expanded by this TRAIN readout.

### Evaluation and interpretation

Complete-chain matching includes sequence identifier, strand and every CDS interval. CDS phase consistency is checked separately. Evaluation uses the fixed CDS-assessable primary policy, selecting the longest eligible CDS per gene and breaking length ties by transcript ID. It retains references outside the model grammar and does not filter them by training eligibility, motif, exon cap or recoverability. Pooled precision/recall/F1 is calculated from pooled counts; per-species, species-macro and worst-species results remain separate. CDS-base unions, duplicate predictions, other-isoform matches and background burdens supplement rather than replace complete-chain accuracy. Reference-relative background is outside annotated gene-feature spans on either strand, not simply outside CDS bases.

ANNEVO, Tiberius and Helixer are evaluated using their fixed native outputs and operating points under the same reference policy. TRAIN results are fitting/development diagnostics. The existing repeatedly used DEV chromosomes are development comparisons, not new independent tests, and upstream exposure is reported by model rather than collapsed into a universal zero-shot claim. Sealed data remain closed, and completed frozen evaluation families are retained without alteration. Actual completed original-model and baseline comparisons remain in the [TRAIN result](../reports/M28-METHOD-RESTART/full_grammar_raw_train_result.md) and [DEV result](../reports/M28-METHOD-RESTART/full_grammar_dev_baseline_result.md).

Supplemental recovery analyses replayed the completed prediction GFF3 against a saved one-to-one profile of the same 22,688 TRAIN primary references. They did not rerun the encoder, alter predictions or filter references. For a fixed control and candidate, references were partitioned into retained hits, gained hits, lost hits and still-missed chains; gained minus lost equaled the whole-evaluation TP difference. Native-gap groups were fixed by the initial update-4,608 model and unchanged native-tool outcomes, not by each candidate. These outcome-defined subsets provide recall accounting, not subset prediction precision or F1.

Additional-fit coverage strata recorded whether at least one sampled core contained the whole reference span with resolved, matching fine labels. Original, fresh and complete-core schedules were joined to the same references before candidate-hit grouping. The coverage flags exclude common starting-fit exposure and do not denote zero partial-label, encoder, partition or upstream exposure. The four-cell context-by-prior comparison used the prespecified F11−F10−F01+F00 contrast on count-derived F1, separately for each species, pooled micro, species macro and worst species. These single-seed TRAIN diagnostics do not provide gene-level independent replicates or mechanistic causality.

The [matched final-update TRAIN score assay](../reports/M28-METHOD-RESTART/full_grammar_matched_energy_diagnostic.md) replays production potentials for structured continuation and joint encoding while retaining their frozen predictions. For a grammar-supported reference g, its selected competitors Cg comprise all same-strand predicted spans with positive overlap; touching spans and opposite strands are not conflicts. The legal single-reference replacement margin is `Eθ(g) − Σh∈Cg Eθ(h)`, using each competitor's entire score rather than truncating it to the overlap. Reference, competitor and margin terms are decomposed into coding body, intronic body, splice coding boundaries and start/stop roles; their sums must reconstruct the production scores. The two models are paired by sequence identifier, strand and full CDS geometry, including unsupported references, and grouped into retained, gained, lost and still-missed hits. Outcome-defined groups are descriptive, and absolute gains from different heads are not calibrated probabilities or comparable confidence scores. The assay does not run a new decoder, change predictions or establish a causal mechanism.

The [native-prior family](../reports/M28-METHOD-RESTART/full_grammar_native_cds_prior_dev_plan.md) and [separate extension](../reports/M28-METHOD-RESTART/full_grammar_sampling_context_dev_plan.md) preserve the unchanged original eight measurements and the complete ten-method results. ### Relation to prior work

Whole-structure conditional likelihood is established in gene prediction: Conrad trains a semi-Markov CRF by conditional maximum likelihood with forward/backward gradients. Neural representations, latent direction/codon states, likelihood training and Viterbi prediction are also present in GeneDecoder, including experiments with pretrained DNA embeddings. Neither a gene-grammar partition function nor a frozen DNA representation followed by structured decoding is sufficient by itself to establish originality. [Conrad Methods](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC1950907/), [GeneDecoder Methods and embedding experiments](https://arxiv.org/html/2505.03377v1).

Sequence-content energies likewise have direct precedents. AUGUSTUS models coding content with interpolated Markov models, reading-frame-dependent components and separate stop-codon probabilities. The earlier Conrad work combines generative features and nonprobabilistic indicators in a discriminatively weighted CRF. The present centered 64-coefficient spliced-CDS term is a specific experiment within this established class of sequence-dependent structural scoring, not a novelty claim based on adding codon scores. [AUGUSTUS original model section](https://gobics.de/mario/papers/GenePred2003.pdf), [Conrad 2006 sections 2 and 3](https://proceedings.neurips.cc/paper_files/paper/2006/file/65a31da7ede4dc9b03fb5bbf8f442ce9-Paper.pdf).

The Carbon-A technical report additionally describes strand-specific coding probabilities, a transition TCN, a sequence-constrained Viterbi gene grammar and a confidence TCN, followed by GFF3 and CDS/protein FASTA output. This is a directly relevant complete-structure precedent, not merely a base-classification model. Its reported pipeline must be distinguished from the narrower released probability-inference examples and from an executed, input-matched baseline. [Carbon-A technical report pages 24 and 25](https://huggingface.co/buckets/HuggingFaceBio/Carbon-A-Technical-Report/resolve/Carbon-A.pdf), [fixed asset and comparison-readiness record](../reports/M28-METHOD-RESTART/carbon_a_pipeline_readiness.md).

The task-specific combination described here—complete-set competition under literal DNA constraints, partially observed fine/coarse labels, base-owned native-window scores and whole-contig output—defines what the experiments test, not a proven first-of-kind algorithm. A paper-level contribution still requires competitive complete-chain performance or independently established applicability, with the corresponding controlled mechanism evidence and computational costs. The [prior-work analysis](M28_complete_chain_competition_prior_work_20261003.md) retains the broader precedents and distinctions. No superiority or independent-generalization claim follows from the implementation alone.
