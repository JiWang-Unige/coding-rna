The arc in one paragraph. From June to September (M1–M27) we tried per-base classifiers with fixed callers, structure heads and several backbones; most failed the cross-species bars we had set. In late September the full CDS grammar (M28) gave a working exact-chain annotator, but on DEV it was below Tiberius and ANNEVO (70.11 against 71.81 and 75.70). On 16–17 October a residual head on frozen Carbon-A outputs (M29) stayed below Carbon-A, and we stopped trying to win on accuracy. The story moved to what the grammar layer can do that the others cannot: train with unknown positions marginalised (M30), and give gene posteriors (M33). Backbones. NT-v2 + LoRA reached 63.61 on DEV but collapsed zero-shot on set B (49.92 / 44.09). PlantCAD2 + LoRA (M35, 72.22; final recipe T2 73.88) closed most of that: set B 68.57 / 68.39. Adding eight training species (T10) did not help (−0.96 DEV, −0.14 set B). Self-training on the target genome (M36) made every arm worse (−1.5 to −7.7). The two decisive tests of November. The ten-plant panel (17 November) failed the C1 criterion: T2's median 55.05 against 67.65 for filtered Carbon-A. P4, the test of C2, held in both seeds (+3.24, +14.97). Then an outside review (ChatGPT, 23 November, checked by me) pointed out that the C2 tests placed the unknown mask using the complete annotation. The oracle-free test M42 (24 November) stopped at gate 1: on a real incomplete annotation, partial-label training is 2.0 points below the zero-shot model. What survived. A grammar layer that trains on any of three backbones; marginalising unknown positions is safer than filling them when the missing positions are known; posteriors that rank gene chains well (AUROC 0.77) though over-confident; and a clear failure mode (genes longer than 10 kb are split).
