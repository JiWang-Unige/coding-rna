What the panel is (reports 10 and 25, PANEL-PREP results). Twelve plant genomes were prepared; the core 10 shown here exclude Chenopodium album (its congener C. quinoa is in PlantCAD2’s pretraining) and the fern Dipteris. Inference uses the main nuclear sequences only: 119 sequences, 4,793 Mb (the NCBI totals, 5.1 Gb, are what the outline quotes). The decision panel-baselines (22 October) fixed the core 10 as the scope. Annotation tiers. TH, strict time hold-out: no RefSeq record before, and the first RefSeq annotation dated on or after 23 February 2026 (Dryas, Eschscholzia, Akebia, Hakea). AH, version hold-out only: Amborella, whose new RefSeq assembly and annotation (September 2026) are held out but whose species is in Carbon-A’s data (older assembly AMTR1.0) and in Helixer’s validation. SH, source hold-out: five species annotated by their submitters (Zizania, Themeda, Juncus, Rhynchospora, Acorus), with undisclosed methods and no BUSCO or RNA-seq qualification yet. Columns. Mb = main nuclear sequence length used. ≥70% id = share of the species’ reference proteins with at least 70% identity to a protein of T2’s training species (Arabidopsis + rice), from the MMseqs2 audit. In Carbon-A = the closest relationship between the panel species and any Carbon-A training species (report 10 §1.7 letters: S same species, G genus, Tr tribe or subfamily, F family, O order only). Things to keep in mind when reading the numbers. 1. Dryas and Eschscholzia contain the set B chromosomes, which T2 has already been scored on (68.57 and 68.39). Two of ten species are therefore partly known. 2. Zizania is in rice’s tribe: 72% of its proteins have a ≥70% match in T2’s training data. It is the most familiar species for T2 and should be expected to score high; report 10 had planned it as the “close relative” layer. 3. Amborella’s species is in Carbon-A’s training data (older assembly), so Carbon-A has an advantage there. Plan 19 and report 10 disagreed on whether Amborella counts in the C1 median; the scored median includes all ten core species. 4. Themeda: 46% of reference proteins have no hit at all, and the submitter lists many more protein-coding genes than usual; low scores there may say more about the reference than the models. 5. Acorus: Tiberius has a species of the same genus in training. 6. Baselines’ training sets were not homology-audited, only listed by species. The rule (next slide) counts every core species except where noted, takes the species median, and compares it with the medians of Helixer, Tiberius and filtered Carbon-A run on the same sequences and scored with our ruler.
