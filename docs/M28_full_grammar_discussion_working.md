# Complete CDS chain annotation Discussion working draft

2026-10-13 (Asia/Shanghai). Complete CDS chain accuracy improved with joint neural encoding, but the completed TRAIN and reused DEV comparisons do not yet establish superiority to the strongest fixed comparators or independently broader applicability. The [Results](M28_full_grammar_results_working.md) support a distinction between the structures a grammar permits, the scores a model learns and the complete chains its caller recovers.

## Structure aware learning and the contribution under test

The implemented model connects partial observations to competition over complete, compatible gene sets, including the empty set. Fine observations restrict the latent roles, coarse observations contribute a fixed observation channel, and unknown positions are marginalized rather than labelled as background. Raw FASTA inference then selects a complete set under the same DNA grammar, using whole-contig scores assembled from finite neural windows. These definitions provide a specific learning problem, not a demonstration that the resulting predictor is more accurate. Training still uses finite-view likelihoods rather than a chromosome-joint likelihood; a whole-contig solve does not make the encoder's context chromosome-wide. The [Methods](M28_full_grammar_methods_working.md) define these distinctions.

Structure-aware neural prediction has direct precedents. Tiberius integrates a differentiable HMM into fine-tuning and inference, while GeneDecoder trains learned representations with a latent CRF and uses Viterbi prediction. Structured training, latent roles and a neural encoder followed by constrained decoding therefore cannot establish novelty on their own. The contribution under test is whether the specified treatment of partial observations and complete-set competition improves the final annotation task. The present comparisons do not establish a first-of-kind method or isolate the benefit of the observation channel. [Tiberius Methods](https://academic.oup.com/bioinformatics/article/40/12/btae685/7903281), [GeneDecoder Methods](https://arxiv.org/html/2505.03377v1).

The fixed-head coarse-observation diagnostic illustrates why an implemented likelihood and a performance mechanism must be distinguished. The first coarse-bearing TRAIN views, containing only one and three coarse rows, had no additional returned logit derivative; maximum-count views from the same unchanged schedule, containing 7,127 and 17,474 rows, did. Uniform-channel entropy constants and fine-only derivatives reconciled in all four views. This establishes that coarse information can affect a local learning signal at this head, not that it improves complete-chain prediction, is necessary across the population or caused the observed accuracy gains. The count-selected follow-up was specified after the first results were known. The [diagnostic and its boundaries](../reports/M28-METHOD-RESTART/full_grammar_coarse_information_probe.md) motivate a matched additional-learning contrast rather than a new claim of mechanism validation.

The completed additional-learning contrast further limits this mechanism claim. Retaining coarse information rather than a uniform channel lowered Arabidopsis chain F1 by 0.908 points and pooled F1 by 0.342 points, while raising rice F1 by 1.537 points. This single-fit contrast starts after 4,608 informative updates; it neither establishes full-training necessity nor justifies deleting the channel or selecting a TRAIN winner for DEV. The [completed result](../reports/M28-METHOD-RESTART/full_grammar_uniform_coarse_result.md) retains the tradeoff. On the historically reused DEV panel, informative minus uniform F1 was +0.312 points pooled, −0.459 in Arabidopsis and +2.084 in rice. The different pooled sign and persistent species tradeoff reinforce the distinction between an active learning signal and a universal accuracy mechanism.

## Complete chain performance and comparator dependence

The initial model's pooled TRAIN CDS-base F1 of 90.360% coexisted with complete-chain F1 of 45.590%. Complete structures require all coding intervals and the strand to match one primary reference, whereas our base endpoint measures the unstranded union of CDS intervals. Neither good coding coverage nor low reference-relative background burden substitutes for this structural endpoint. The matched structured-versus-local continuation contrast reinforces that distinction: the structured objective had higher complete-chain F1 despite lower CDS-base F1, with the same final caller. This is an objective comparison, not evidence that the caller is necessary or that one module caused the entire improvement.

Joint encoding attained pooled chain F1 of 66.963% on TRAIN and 66.623% on the reused DEV chromosomes. Its DEV F1 exceeded the fixed Helixer comparator in both species, but the pooled advantage combined 7.806-percentage-point higher precision with 0.543-point lower recall. It recovered 42 fewer primary chains than Helixer overall, including 134 fewer in rice. It also remained 5.182 points below Tiberius and 9.080 below ANNEVO pooled. Thus the observed gain is meaningful for this complete-chain operating point without demonstrating universal superiority, uniformly greater recovery or a new state of the art. The [fixed DEV comparison](../reports/M28-METHOD-RESTART/full_grammar_sampling_context_dev_plan.md#十方法扩展-dev-终态与结果) retains every method and the unchanged reference denominator.

Native Carbon-A provides both a complete-structure precedent and a completed original-TRAIN pipeline comparison. Its fixed filtered and unfiltered outputs reached pooled chain F1 of 78.172% and 76.063%, respectively, compared with 66.963% for the completed joint-encoding candidate. The current candidate therefore remains below this strong complete-pipeline comparator. The fixed confidence filter increased F1 while losing 127 primary hits, emphasizing the distinction between endpoint balance and greater recovery. Carbon-A's preserved main weights share the annotation-pretrained source used here, but its context, native CDS heads, learned transition and confidence networks, grammar and filtering differ. Shared main weights therefore do not isolate a decoder effect. Unfiltered predictions still depend on the learned transition network, and each auxiliary network's training and validation exposure must be considered separately from the main-model assembly list. This TRAIN-only comparison is not part of the frozen DEV family and does not establish independent generalization, clean zero-shot prediction or matched computational efficiency. The [completed comparison](../reports/M28-METHOD-RESTART/carbon_a_original_train_result.md) and [component-specific exposure record](../reports/M28-METHOD-RESTART/pretrained_exposure.md) define these boundaries.

The paired TRAIN diagnostic concentrates the current deficit in multisegment references: they contributed 95.279% of filtered Carbon-A's net additional primary hits, while only one net hit came from a reference spanning more than the 24,576-base joint window. This makes recovery of complete multisegment structures a concrete endpoint for interpreting the ongoing original-scope fits; it does not establish that context is irrelevant or that any particular scoring component causes the gap. The 590 filtered-Carbon-A misses recovered by joint encoding show nonidentical reference-hit sets, not a deployable ensemble or newly discovered genes. Both fixed native operating points remain reported without threshold selection.

The native window-policy intervention offers a narrower test of the context hypothesis. Reducing Carbon-A's window to 24,576 bases lowered filtered and unfiltered pooled F1 by 0.609 and 0.478 percentage points, respectively, with declines on every chromosome, but left gaps of 10.600 and 8.622 points over the completed joint candidate. The tested window-policy change therefore did not remove most of the observed pipeline separation. Because absolute overlap was fixed, relative overlap, window placement and averaging geometry changed together; these differences cannot be attributed to context length alone. Neither a causal fraction of the cross-pipeline gap nor the outcome of extending the grammar model's window can be inferred from this [TRAIN-only result](../reports/M28-METHOD-RESTART/carbon_a_context24_train_result.md).

The primary-chain endpoint also needs to be distinguished from annotation support more generally. The default filtered native output included 1,365 exact matches to other assessable isoforms that remained nonprimary benchmark residuals; the completed joint output included 1,267. Such matches are annotation-relative evidence for CDS geometry, not independent biological validation. Conversely, matching neither exact reference category does not prove a predicted gene false. These global unique-prediction counts cannot explain which primary references were lost or establish same-gene alternative recovery, and they do not change the fixed comparison or justify post hoc endpoint selection.

The completed sampling-by-encoding contrast strengthens the within-panel result without closing the competitive gap. Fresh-joint reached pooled TRAIN chain F1 of 70.303%, improving over original-joint by 3.340 points and exceeding Helixer in both species, but still falling below Tiberius, ANNEVO and the native Carbon-A operating points. The pooled interaction was −3.988 points on the nonlinear F1 scale, so the two observed improvements should not be described as superadditive complementarity. A decrease in CDS-base F1 and an increase in background burden also caution against equating higher exact-chain F1 with uniformly cleaner annotations. The separate frozen DEV comparison subsequently reached 70.107% pooled F1 for fresh-joint, exceeding Helixer in both species but remaining below Tiberius and ANNEVO. This historically reused development result narrows the observed gap without establishing independently broader applicability.

Low-rank encoder adaptation further narrowed the original-TRAIN gap: pooled complete-chain F1 reached 72.958%, 2.656 points above fresh-joint, with gains in both species. It slightly exceeded Tiberius pooled while remaining lower in Arabidopsis, and still fell below ANNEVO and the default Carbon-A pipelines. Increased background burden and lower CDS-base precision temper the chain-recovery gain. This supports a conditional performance benefit of the specified adaptation, not an isolated capacity or optimization-age mechanism, uniform state-of-the-art performance or independent generalization. The [completed adaptation result](../reports/M28-METHOD-RESTART/full_grammar_joint_lora_result.md) stays TRAIN-only and does not enter the frozen DEV family.

The completed full-rank q/v continuation also improved the frozen fresh-joint TRAIN control on every chromosome, reaching pooled F1 of 72.200%, but remained below Tiberius pooled and ANNEVO in both species. It was 0.758 points below the rank-four intervention pooled, while having lower background burden and slightly higher CDS-base F1. The two fixed settings therefore exhibit different endpoint balances; they do not isolate the causal effect of rank, because learning rate, parameterization, forward rounding and optimization trajectories also differ.

Read-only reference replay further showed that the full-rank predictor was not simply a subset of the low-rank predictor: it retained 15,636 shared hits, gained 470 and lost 784. Most net losses occurred among multisegment references, but these post-outcome bins are descriptive recall accounting and do not establish why the structures were lost. Neither overlapping reference-hit sets nor lower background coverage provide a reference-free ensemble rule or independent biological validation. The [completed full-rank record](../reports/M28-METHOD-RESTART/full_grammar_dense_qv_result.md) preserves this tradeoff without extending DEV scope or selecting subsequent training.

In contrast, complete-spliced-CDS codon energies lowered pooled TRAIN chain F1 by 4.834 points, with declines on every chromosome, fewer primary hits and more predictions. Verified structural scoring and added parameters therefore did not guarantee improved recovery. The [completed negative contrast](../reports/M28-METHOD-RESTART/full_grammar_codon_result.md) changes the trained head and its optimization trajectory as well as adding content energies, so it cannot alone attribute the loss to individual codon coefficients or disprove coding-content modeling generally. It was retained in the frozen DEV design, where its pooled F1 was 5.528 points below fresh-joint, with declines in both species. Agreement across these fitting and reused-development views does not make the intervention a clean causal isolation or an independent test. The [complete fixed-family result](../reports/M28-METHOD-RESTART/full_grammar_joint_controls_dev_result.md) preserves the negative branch.

## Structural capacity and learned score competition

The reference-informed TRAIN compatibility oracle could retain 22,460 of 22,688 primary chains within the implemented grammar and same-strand nonoverlap constraint. Its 99.4950% F1 ceiling assumes access to the references and no additional predictions; it is not achievable model accuracy established by raw FASTA. Nevertheless, the 209 individually unsupported references and 19 further compatibility losses cannot, by themselves, explain the much larger learned accuracy deficit. Grammar capacity and learning success are separate questions.

The matched final-update score replay narrows the descriptive account of that deficit. Of joint encoding's 7,762 missed TRAIN references, 2,486 had negative energy, 5,067 had positive energy but were outscored by their complete selected conflict sets, and 209 were individually unsupported. Of the 2,956 references newly recovered relative to structured continuation, 2,123 had previously been positive but outscored; 833 had been negative. Another 1,085 changed from negative to positive-but-outscored without recovery. These migrations show why a reduction in the negative-energy count alone cannot quantify recovered genes. No legal single-reference replacement strictly improved either frozen selected set, which supports scoring consistency rather than independently proving global MAP optimality. The [matched replay](../reports/M28-METHOD-RESTART/full_grammar_matched_energy_diagnostic.md#完整终态与评分结果) preserves the complete paired population.

When gained chains were scored with the old structured head and lost or jointly missed supported chains with the joint head, intron terms were the largest negative mean contribution. This is an arithmetic feature of unnormalized reference-minus-conflict score sums, not a causal diagnosis of intron modeling. Chain length, role exposure and conflict geometry differ, the groups are defined by prediction outcomes, and absolute score scales across trained heads are not calibrated. An exact selected chain's zero replacement margin is a self-comparison, not an explanation of recovery. Consequently, the replay does not justify changing intron weights or claim that such a change would repair missed chains.

## Sampling and additional evidence are not additive guarantees

Neither more complete-core supervision nor the combination of native CDS priors with joint encoding improved reference recovery uniformly. The complete-core proposal increased directly observed complete-chain coverage but lost net hits relative to fresh uniform sampling, while the prior-and-context combination had lower complete-chain F1 than either component alone. Importance correction matches an expected objective, not finite exposure, clipped gradients or AdamW trajectories. Likewise, matching update counts does not match effective optimization or upstream annotation exposure. The existing four-cell prior-and-context contrast is descriptive and does not establish mechanistic antagonism. The completed fresh-sampling-by-joint-encoding comparison also had a negative interaction on the nonlinear F1 scale. None of these single-fit results establishes general causal antagonism or expands the frozen DEV family.

## Biological scope and independent evidence

The model currently annotates complete protein-coding CDS chains, not UTRs, noncoding genes or every alternative isoform. All unsupported references remain in evaluation, and all seven earlier M28 weights and all three new fixed candidates missed the three DEV references spanning more than a native read window. Whole-contig decoding alone therefore establishes neither long-gene recovery nor broader feature coverage. Similarly, single-fit measurements from two species and historically reused DEV chromosomes cannot establish broad taxonomic applicability or clean zero-shot generalization. The annotation-pretrained backbone retains upstream supervision even when frozen and separated from its original classifier; exposure isolation remains model-dependent. The [exposure record](../reports/M28-METHOD-RESTART/pretrained_exposure.md) identifies the known and unresolved boundaries.

A stronger performance or applicability claim requires independent evaluation with explicit sequence and homology isolation, relevant comparator versions and complete-chain denominators, together with controlled evidence for the proposed mechanism. Repeatability and matched computational measurements would address different questions from accuracy; the current execution timings do not establish an efficiency advantage. The present results motivate continued investigation of complete-set scoring under partial observations, but do not yet satisfy those broader claims.
