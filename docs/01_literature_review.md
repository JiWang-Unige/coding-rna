## 1. Research question

The project targets eukaryotic ab initio protein-coding gene annotation from raw DNA sequence. The model should produce per-base multi-class labels and ultimately support whole-gene annotation quality, with strong cross-species generalization. The immediate evaluation preference is to control intergenic false positives while measuring gene-body F1; once performance approaches SOTA, gene-level / locus-level F1 becomes a required secondary claim metric.

The project aims for a Nature Methods-level contribution, so pure tuning, small window classification, or ensemble-only improvements are not sufficient. The expected contribution should involve architecture, decoding, objective, data view, or foundation-model adaptation that improves full annotation behavior under comparable benchmarks.

## 2. Scope and task formulation

- Input: raw genomic DNA sequence only.
- Output: per-base multi-class labels for protein-coding gene annotation. The exact label set is not fixed yet; candidates include gene-body / intergenic, exon / intron / intergenic, CDS / UTR / intron / intergenic, and strand-aware labels.
- In scope: ab initio protein-coding gene annotation; whole-genome or whole-chromosome inference; held-out species evaluation; gene-body and intergenic boundary quality; downstream conversion to gene models where applicable.
- Out of scope: RNA-seq/protein homology evidence pipelines as the main task; RNA folding / 3D RNA; promoter/enhancer/TF-binding/ncRNA-only annotation; transcript classification only; private non-reproducible benchmarks.
- Important benchmark constraint: random sequence splits are not acceptable. The target benchmark should use species-level, chromosome-level, or homology-aware splits, with explicit safeguards against paralog/homology leakage.

## 5. Datasets and metrics

### Datasets

- RefSeq multi-species eukaryotic annotations: repeatedly mentioned for Tiberius / ANNEVO-like workflows. Need exact release, species list, genome builds, transcript collapsing rule, and train/test species.
- Ensembl / Ensembl Genomes: likely central for broad eukaryotic benchmarks. Need exact release, clade coverage, annotation quality filters, and held-out species list.
- GENCODE human/mouse: useful high-quality human/mouse reference, but not sufficient alone for cross-species SOTA. May be useful for focused human evaluation.
- SegmentNT human and multi-species segmentation data: useful for foundation-model representation and CDS/gene-element probing, but not automatically comparable to full gene-caller benchmarks.
- GENERanno eukaryotic CDS annotation data: high-priority to inspect because user wants to try this model early, but current external reports suggest weak public documentation for true eukaryotic benchmark details.

Dataset risks to verify:
- Species-level leakage and homology leakage.
- Chromosome-only splits that overestimate cross-species performance.
- Transcript isoform collapsing differences.
- Whether UTRs are included or evaluation is CDS-only.
- Whether soft-masking / repeat masking is used.
- Reference annotation noise, especially in non-model species.

### Metrics

Candidate primary metric:
- Whole-genome or whole-chromosome gene-body F1 under strong intergenic false-positive control.

Candidate secondary and claim metrics:
- Gene-level / locus-level F1 after performance approaches SOTA.
- Exon / intron / CDS precision, recall, and F1.
- Intron-chain F1.
- Splice donor/acceptor precision and recall.
- Boundary error distribution.
- Fragmentation and fusion counts.
- BUSCO completeness as a supportive metric only, not a primary FP-aware metric.

Open metric issues:
- Gene-level F1 may mean exact transcript chain match, locus-level gffcompare match, CDS-only gene match, or full gene body match. This must be nailed down before any claim.
- SegmentNT-style MCC/F1 for genomic elements cannot be directly compared to Tiberius/Helixer/ANNEVO gene-caller F1.
- GENERanno preview may report CDS/token-level metrics rather than full gene-model metrics.

## 6. Research gaps (from reports + user input)

- Current strongest gene-caller methods appear to rely on traditional neural backbones plus structured decoding rather than pure foundation models. This suggests the main gap may be decoding/objective/benchmark alignment, not simply model scale.
- Long-context modeling remains unresolved for long introns, long genes, distant gene boundaries, and fragmentation/fusion errors.
- Intergenic false positives are central to the user-defined task; existing reports warn that BUSCO or local window metrics can hide FP problems.
- Foundation models such as SegmentNT and GENERanno may provide useful CDS/splice/gene-element signals, but current evidence suggests they are not yet clean full-gene annotation SOTA under comparable benchmarks.
- Biological constraints such as GT/AG splice motifs, start/stop codons, ORF consistency, strand consistency, and phase transitions remain valuable and should be integrated into model design or decoding rather than used only as ad hoc post-filtering.
- Benchmark fragmentation is itself a gap: ANNEVO/Tiberius/Helixer/SegmentNT/GENERanno may use different labels, data sources, split units, and metric definitions.

## 7. Architecture opportunities (针对我们的具体方向)

1. **Structured decoder / objective for full gene consistency**
   Build a decoder/head that explicitly models gene-body, exon/intron/CDS state transitions, strand, phase, start/stop, and GT/AG splice constraints. This is first-priority because the top reported full gene callers (ANNEVO, Tiberius, Helixer) are not simply foundation models; they all use structured decoding or gene grammar in some form. Candidate mechanisms: differentiable HMM, CRF, semi-CRF, latent-CRF, constrained Viterbi, FP-aware decoding thresholds, or trainable transition potentials.

2. **Long-context backbone for long genes and fragmentation/fusion control**
   Explore a backbone that can handle substantially longer context than local segmentation windows, targeting long introns, very long genes, and intergenic false positives. Candidate mechanisms: dilated convolution, state-space/Mamba-like models, hierarchical chunk aggregation, recurrent memory, or sparse/linear attention. This should be tested against long-gene strata, not just aggregate F1.

3. **FP-aware gene-body objective and hard-negative mining**
   Design training objectives that penalize intergenic false positives and fragmented/fused gene calls under whole-genome evaluation. Candidate mechanisms: intergenic hard negatives, repeat/TE-aware auxiliary labels, boundary-aware losses, calibration losses, gene-body precision constraint, and post-hoc threshold selection validated only on held-out species.

4. **Foundation-model adaptation as a separate but not first-priority path**
   Run SegmentNT and GENERanno early to inspect their raw annotation behavior, especially for CDS and gene-body signals. Treat them first as pretrained representation/backbone candidates and secondary baselines, not as primary gene-caller SOTA anchors. If their raw predictions show useful signal, add a structured decoder and compare whether foundation-model features improve over Tiberius/Helixer-like traditional backbones.

5. **Biological-constraint-aware post-processing / filtering**
   Use classical gene-prediction constraints as a disciplined, ablated component: GT/AG splice donor/acceptor signals, start/stop codons, no premature stop codon, ORF length/phase consistency, minimum intron/exon length, strand consistency. This must be evaluated by ablation; it should not become an unverified heuristic layer that only improves validation by leakage.
